$H^0$ is the unperturbed Hamiltonian and $H^\prime$ is the perturbation.

\textbf{(a)}
\begin{lefteqs}
    H = \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta & \lambda & \lambda \\
        \lambda & \Delta & \lambda \\
        \lambda & \lambda & 0 \\
    \end{pmatrix}
    \quad
    ,
    \quad
    H \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        -1 \\
        0 \\
    \end{pmatrix}
    = \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta - \lambda \\
        \lambda - \Delta \\
        0
    \end{pmatrix}
    = E_0 \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        -1 \\
        0
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{lefteqs}

Using the hint the first eigenvalue is $E_0 = \Delta - \lambda$

Since H is symmetric (Hermitian) it has an orthonomral basis of eigenvectors, where the hinted
$(1,-1,0)$ is one of them, and the other two eigenvalues will be orthogonal to it.

A vector $(a,b,c)$ which is orthogonal to $(1,-1,0)$ has the form:
\begin{lefteqs}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        a & b & c
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        -1 \\
        0
    \end{pmatrix}
    = a-b=0 \implies a=b \implies (a,a,c) \\
    H \begin{pmatrix}
        a \\
        a \\
        c
    \end{pmatrix}
    = \begin{pmatrix}
        a \Delta + a \lambda + \lambda c \\
        a \Delta + a \lambda + \lambda c \\
        a \lambda + a \lambda + 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    = \begin{pmatrix}
        a (\Delta + \lambda) + \lambda c \\
        a (\Delta + \lambda) + \lambda c \\
        2 \lambda a
    \end{pmatrix}
    = E \begin{pmatrix}
        a \\
        a \\
        c
    \end{pmatrix} \\
    a(\Delta + \lambda) + c \lambda = E a \implies 2a \lambda = Ec \implies c = \frac{2 \lambda a}{E} \\
    a(\Delta + \lambda) + \frac{2a \lambda}{E} \lambda = Ea \\
    \Delta + \lambda + \frac{2 \lambda^2}{E} = E 
    \implies E^2 - E(\Delta + \lambda) - 2 \lambda^2 = 0 \\
    E_{\pm} = \frac{(\Delta + \lambda) \pm \sqrt{(\Delta + \lambda)^2 + 8 \lambda^2}}{2}
\end{lefteqs}

The exact eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian are $E_0$ and $E_{\pm}$.

\textbf{(a)}

Given Hamiltonian $H(\lambda)$ and $E_n(\lambda)$ its eigenvalues and $\psi_n(\lambda)$ its eigenfunctions.

We need to prove: $\dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} = \Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n}$

\begin{lefteqs}
    H \ket{\psi_n} = E_n \ket{\psi_n} \\
    \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial \lambda} \ket{\psi_n} + H \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda}
    = \dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} \ket{\psi_n} + E_n \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda} \\
    \Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n} + \bra{\psi_n} H \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda}
    = \Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n} + \bra{\psi_n} E_n \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda} \\
    \Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n} + \left( \bra{\psi_n} H \right) \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda}
    = \Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n} + E_n \bra{\psi_n} \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda}
\end{lefteqs}

And since $H$ is Hermitian (so $E_n$ is real):

\begin{lefteqs}
    H \ket{\psi_n} = E_n \ket{\psi_n} \implies \bra{\psi_n} H = E_n \bra{\psi_n} \implies \left( \bra{\psi_n} H \right) \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda} = E_n \bra{\psi_n} \dfrac{\partial \ket{\psi_n}}{\partial \lambda}
\end{lefteqs}

Also, since $\dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda}$ is a scalar: $\Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n} = \dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} \braket{\psi_n | \psi_n} = \dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda}$

And so we get $\dfrac{\partial E_n}{\partial \lambda} = \Braket{\psi_n | \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial \lambda} | \psi_n}$

\begin{lefteqs}
    E_n = \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar \omega \\
    \lambda = \omega : \quad \frac{\partial E_n}{\partial \omega} = \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar
    \quad , \quad
    \frac{\partial H}{\partial \omega} = m \omega x^2 = \frac{2}{\omega} \cdot \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2 = \frac{2V}{\omega}
\end{lefteqs}

Applying Feynman-Hellmann:

\begin{lefteqs}
    \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar = \left\langle \frac{2V}{\omega} \right\rangle = \frac{2}{\omega} \langle V \rangle
    \implies \langle V \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar \omega = \frac{E_n}{2} \\
    \lambda = \hbar : \quad \frac{\partial E_n}{\partial \hbar} = \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \omega
\end{lefteqs}

\begin{lefteqs}
    \text{Since } \hat{p} = -i \hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \implies p^2 = -\hbar^2 \frac{d^2}{dx^2}
    \implies H = \left( -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \right) \frac{d^2}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2 \\
    \frac{\partial H}{\partial \hbar} = \left( \frac{-\hbar}{m} \right) \cdot \frac{d^2}{dx^2}
    = \frac{2}{\hbar} \cdot \frac{-\hbar^2}{2m} \cdot \frac{d^2}{dx^2} = \frac{2}{\hbar} \cdot T
\end{lefteqs}

Applying Feynman-Hellmann:

\begin{lefteqs}
    \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \omega = \left\langle \frac{2}{\hbar} T \right\rangle = \frac{2}{\hbar} \langle T \rangle
    \implies \langle T \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \omega \hbar = \frac{E_n}{2} \\
    \lambda = m : \quad \frac{\partial E_n}{\partial m} = 0
    \quad , \quad
    \frac{\partial H}{\partial m} = \frac{-p^2}{2m^2} + \frac{1}{2} \omega^2 x^2
    = \frac{1}{m} \left( \frac{-p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2 \right) \\
    \quad = \frac{1}{m} \left( V - T \right) \\
    0 = \left\langle \frac{1}{m} (V - T) \right\rangle = \frac{1}{m} \left( \langle V \rangle - \langle T \rangle \right)
    \implies \langle V \rangle = \langle T \rangle
\end{lefteqs}

Problem 2.12 gives $\langle T \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar \omega = \frac{E_n}{2}$, as we just got.

The virial theorem (Problem 3.37) finds that $\langle V \rangle = \langle T \rangle$, just as we got in this problem.

(Problem 7.42 from course book)

\textbf{(a)}
Equation 4.53 gives the effective Hamiltonian for the radial wave functions:

\begin{lefteqs}
    H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \cdot \frac{d^2}{dr^2} + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \cdot \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} - \frac{e^2}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} \cdot \frac{1}{r}
\end{lefteqs}

And equation 4.70 gives the eigenvalues:

\begin{lefteqs}
    E_n = -\frac{m e^4}{32 \pi^2 \epsilon_0^2 \hbar^2 (N + \ell)^2}
\end{lefteqs}

We'll use $\lambda = e$ in the Feynman-Hellmann theorem.

\begin{lefteqs}
    \frac{\partial E_n}{\partial e} = -\frac{m e^3}{8 \pi^2 \epsilon_0^2 \hbar^2 (N + \ell)^2} \\
    \frac{\partial H}{\partial e} = -\frac{e}{2 \pi \epsilon_0} \cdot \frac{1}{r} \\
    -\frac{m e^3}{8 \pi^2 \epsilon_0^2 \hbar^2 (N + \ell)^2} = \left\langle \frac{-e}{2 \pi \epsilon_0} \cdot \frac{1}{r} \right\rangle = \frac{-e}{2 \pi \epsilon_0} \left\langle \frac{1}{r} \right\rangle \\
    \left\langle \frac{1}{r} \right\rangle = \frac{m e^2}{4 \pi \epsilon_0 \hbar^2 (N + \ell)^2} = \frac{1}{(N + \ell)^2 a} = \frac{1}{n^2 a}
\end{lefteqs}

This result agrees with 7.56
