\textbf{\large 1.}
\begin{lefteqs}
    H = H^0 + H^\prime \\
    H^0 = \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & \Delta & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \quad
    H^\prime = \lambda \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & 1 & 1 \\
        1 & 0 & 1 \\
        1 & 1 & 0 \\
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{lefteqs}
$H^0$ is the unperturbed Hamiltonian and $H^\prime$ is the perturbation.

\textbf{(a)}
\begin{lefteqs}
    H = \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta & \lambda & \lambda \\
        \lambda & \Delta & \lambda \\
        \lambda & \lambda & 0 \\
    \end{pmatrix}
    \quad
    ,
    \quad
    H \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        -1 \\
        0 \\
    \end{pmatrix}
    = \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta - \lambda \\
        \lambda - \Delta \\
        0
    \end{pmatrix}
    = E_0 \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        -1 \\
        0
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{lefteqs}

Using the hint the first eigenvalue is $E_0 = \Delta - \lambda$

Since H is symmetric (Hermitian) it has an orthonomral basis of eigenvectors, where the hinted
$(1,-1,0)$ is one of them, and the other two eigenvalues will be orthogonal to it.

A vector $(a,b,c)$ which is orthogonal to $(1,-1,0)$ has the form:
\begin{lefteqs}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        a & b & c
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        -1 \\
        0
    \end{pmatrix}
    = a-b=0 \implies a=b \implies (a,a,c) \\
    H \begin{pmatrix}
        a \\
        a \\
        c
    \end{pmatrix}
    = \begin{pmatrix}
        a \Delta + a \lambda + \lambda c \\
        a \Delta + a \lambda + \lambda c \\
        a \lambda + a \lambda + 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    = \begin{pmatrix}
        a (\Delta + \lambda) + \lambda c \\
        a (\Delta + \lambda) + \lambda c \\
        2 \lambda a
    \end{pmatrix}
    = E \begin{pmatrix}
        a \\
        a \\
        c
    \end{pmatrix} \\
    a(\Delta + \lambda) + c \lambda = E a \implies 2a \lambda = Ec \implies c = \frac{2 \lambda a}{E} \\
    a(\Delta + \lambda) + \frac{2a \lambda}{E} \lambda = Ea \\
    \Delta + \lambda + \frac{2 \lambda^2}{E} = E 
    \implies E^2 - E(\Delta + \lambda) - 2 \lambda^2 = 0 \\
    E_{\pm} = \frac{(\Delta + \lambda) \pm \sqrt{(\Delta + \lambda)^2 + 8 \lambda^2}}{2}
\end{lefteqs}

The exact eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian are $E_0$ and $E_{\pm}$.

\textbf{(c)}
First, we calculate the matrix elements of W.
According to equation 7.28:
\begin{lefteqs}
    W_{ij} = \langle \psi^0_i | H' | \psi^0_j \rangle

In our case the stationary states for the degenerate energy level are
$e_1 = (1,0,0)$ and $e_2 = (0,1,0)$.

\begin{lefteqs}
    \implies W_{ij} = \langle e_i^T | H' | e_j \rangle \quad , i,j \in \{1,2\} \\

    = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \lambda \\ 0 \\ \lambda \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \\

\begin{lefteqs}
    \text{(7.33)} \quad E^1_{\pm} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ W_{aa} + W_{bb} \pm \sqrt{(W_{aa} - W_{bb})^2 + 4 |W_{ab}|^2} \right] \\
    \implies  E^1_{\pm} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ 0 + 0 \pm \sqrt{(0 - 0)^2 + 4 |\lambda|^2} \right] = \pm \lambda
\end{lefteqs}

The first order corrections to the energy of the degenerate level are $E^1_{\pm} = \pm \lambda$,
so the degenerate level splits into $\Delta + \lambda$ and $\Delta - \lambda$

The "good" states are the eigenvectors of W:

\begin{lefteqs}
    W \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \pm \lambda \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \\
    \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \lambda \\ \lambda & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} b \\ a \end{pmatrix}
    = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \implies a=b \\
    \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \lambda \\ \lambda & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = -\lambda \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \\
    \begin{pmatrix} \lambda b \\ \lambda a \end{pmatrix} = -\lambda \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \implies a=-b
\end{lefteqs}

The good states are $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_1 +  e_2)$ with first order energy correction $\lambda$
 and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_1 -  e_2)$ with first order energy correction $-\lambda$

\textbf{(d)}
The first order correction to the energy of the non-degenerate levelis given by equation 7.9:
\begin{lefteqs}
    \text{(7.9)} \quad E^1_n = \braket{\psi^0_n | H' | \psi^0_n} \\
    \implies E^1_n = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} H' \begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \lambda \\ \lambda \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = 0
\end{lefteqs}

The second order correction is given by 7.15:

\begin{lefteqs}
    \text{(7.15)} \quad E^2_n = \displaystyle \sum_{n \neq m} \frac{|\braket{\psi^0_m | H' | \psi^0_n}|^2}{E^0_n - E^0_m} \\
    E^0_n = E^0_3 = 0 \quad \text{(part b)} \\
    E^0_m = E^0_1 = E^0_2 = \Delta \quad \text{(part b)} \\
    \braket{e_1 | H' | e_3} = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix} H' \begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \lambda \\ \lambda \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \\
    \braket{e_2 | H' | e_3} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 & 0\end{pmatrix} H' \begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 & 0\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \lambda \\ \lambda \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \\
    E^2_3 = \frac{|\lambda|^2}{0 - \Delta} + \frac{|\lambda|^2}{0 - \Delta} = -\frac{2\lambda^2}{\Delta}
\end{lefteqs}

The first order correction to the energy of the non degenerate level is $E^1_3 = 0$ and the second order is $E^2_3 = -\frac{2\lambda^2}{\Delta}$
